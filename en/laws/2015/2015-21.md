# Law No. (21) of 2015 on the Regulation of Entry, Exit, and Residency of Expatriates

We, Tamim Bin Hamad Al Thani, Amir of the State of Qatar,

After reviewing the Constitution,

and the Labour Law issued by Law / Act No. (14) of 2004, and its amending laws,

and Law / Act No. (4) of 2009 on the Regulation of the entry, exit, residence, and sponsorship of expatriates,

upon the proposal of the Minister of Interior,

and upon the Draft Law submitted by the Cabinet / Council of Ministers,

and after taking the opinion of the Shura Council,

We have decided the following Law / Act:

## Chapter Two: Entry and Exit of Expatriates (2-7)

### Article 2

A foreigner may not enter or leave the State unless he holds a valid passport or travel document and has obtained an entry visa from the Competent Authority, indicating the purpose of entry.

The Ruling of the previous paragraph does not apply in the case of departure.

### Article 3

The expatriate may not enter or leave the State except through the entry and exit points designated by the Minister, and after placing the entry or exit stamp on his passport or travel document, or by any other mechanism determined by the Minister.

### Article 4

The Competent Authority, or any other authority designated by the Minister, shall issue entry permits In accordance with the provisions of this Law, and any such permits may be cancelled after issuance for reasons related to the public interest.

Entry permits for expatriates for the purpose of work shall not be granted except under an employment contract concluded with their recruiter in accordance with the conditions and controls prescribed by law.

It is prohibited to transfer the permits to others, to dispose of them in any way, or for them to be traded by others, whether the transfer, disposition, or trading is for consideration or without consideration.

The Executive Regulations of this Law shall specify the conditions and controls for granting those permits, as well as the specific duration for each of them.

### Article 5

Ship captains, aircraft commanders, vehicle drivers, and operators of other means of transport must, upon arrival in the State and before departure, submit to the Competent Authority a list of the names of their crew and passengers, along with their relevant information. They must also report to the relevant authorities any passengers who do not hold valid passports, travel documents, or entry visas, and prevent them from leaving the ship or aircraft, as appropriate.

In all cases, the carrier is obliged, at its own expense, to return any passenger who does not possess a passport, travel document, or entry visa to the country from which they arrived or to their country of nationality.

### Article 6

Managers of Hospitality Establishments and tourism establishments, or their representatives, must provide information to the Competent Authority about persons who have been allowed to enter the State through them. They must accommodate them in the location where their permits were issued, unless necessity requires otherwise. In the event that any of them is absent from the place of residence for more than forty-eight hours without notifying the Hospitality Establishment or tourism establishment, the Competent Authority must be informed within the following twenty-four hours. The Hospitality Establishment or tourism establishment assumes all the obligations of the sponsor towards the visitor.

In all cases, anyone who accommodates a visitor must inform the Department / Directorate of security in whose jurisdiction the Hospitality Establishment or tourism establishment or place of accommodation is located, of the visitor’s name and address within twenty-four hours of their arrival.

### Article 7 Amended

The expatriate subject to the Labor Law referred to, has the right to temporary exit or final departure from the country during the validity of the employment contract. The recruiter may submit a justified and prior request to the Ministry of Administrative Development, Labor and Social Affairs, listing the names of those whom he deems, due to the nature of their work, require prior approval for leaving the country, not exceeding (5%) of the number of his employees. If the Ministry of Administrative Development, Labor and Social Affairs approves the request, the Competent Authority shall be notified.

As for expatriates not subject to the Labor Law referred to, the Controls and Procedures for their exit from the country shall be determined by a decision from the minister.

If the expatriate is prevented from leaving the country for any reason, he may resort to the Committee for Expatriate Exit Grievances, which is formed and its competencies, procedures to be followed before it, and its operational system are determined by a decision from the minister.

The Committee must decide on the Raising a Grievance within three working days.

## Chapter Three: Residence of Expatriates in the State (8-16)

### Article 8

Every expatriate who wishes to reside in the State for any purpose must obtain a License from the Competent Authority.

The sponsor is obligated to carry out the procedures for the License and its renewal within a period not exceeding ninety days from the date of its expiration.

The Employer must hand over the expatriate’s passport or travel document after completing the procedures for the License or its renewal, unless the expatriate requests in writing that the Employer retains it on his behalf, provided that it is handed over to him upon request.

### Article 9

The Competent Authority issues the residence permit to the expatriate In accordance with the provisions of this Law, and it shall bear his name, photograph, signature, and personal identity data as determined by a decision from the Minister.

### Article 10

The applicant must enable the expatriate to visit the competent authorities within thirty days from the date the expatriate entered the State, to complete the procedures for obtaining a residence or visit permit, and the expatriate may not refuse to complete these procedures.

The competent authority may authorize the applicant or the expatriate to delegate someone to carry out some of the procedures mentioned in the previous paragraph on their behalf.

### Article 11

The expatriate who enters the State for the purpose of visiting, or for other purposes for a period not exceeding thirty days, is exempt from the obligations stipulated in the previous article.

The expatriate may not remain in the State after the expiration of the period mentioned, unless it is renewed or he obtains residency.

### Article 12

The competent authority may grant residence Licenses to the spouse of the residence License holder and to his male children who have not completed their university studies up to the age of twenty-five, as well as to his unmarried daughters.

The Minister or his delegate may make exceptions to this age limit, and with the approval of the Minister or his delegate, a residence License may also be granted to the parents of the License holder if there is a justified reason.

The competent authority may grant a residence License to the spouse and children of a Qatari national who is married to a non-Qatari, in accordance with the law, and it is also permissible to grant a residence License to his non-Qatari parents.

The conditions for granting a residence License, in accordance with the provisions of this article, shall be specified by a decision of the Minister.

### Article 13

The expatriate who has been licensed for his family to reside must apply for a residence permit for his newborn within ninety days from the date of birth or entry into the State.

If the birth occurs outside the State and the parents or one of them has a valid residence permit, the newborn is authorized to enter within six months from the date of birth, and the competent authority may waive this period.

The sponsor of a female expatriate visiting must apply to amend the status of her child born inside the State within sixty days from the date of birth.

The granting of residence permits to children of expatriates born inside the State shall be in accordance with the conditions and regulations issued by a decision of the minister.

### Article 14

The expatriate who has been granted a residence permit may not remain outside the State continuously for more than six months, unless he obtains, before his departure or before one year has passed since his exit, permission to return from the Competent Authority, after paying the prescribed fees, provided that no more than sixty days have passed since the expiry of his residence.

The Minister, or his delegate, may waive the periods stipulated in the previous paragraph.

### Article 15

During his stay in the State, the expatriate must present his passport or travel document or residence permit to the Competent Authority whenever requested, and provide any information he may be asked for, within the specified time.

In the event of loss or damage to the passport, travel document, or residence permit, the expatriate must notify the Competent Authority immediately upon discovering the loss or damage, and obtain a replacement for the lost or damaged document.

### Article 16

A foreigner who is licensed to enter or reside for a specific purpose, or to work for a particular entity, must not violate the purpose for which he was licensed, leave the employment of his sponsor, or work for an entity not authorized for him.

It is not considered a violation of the purpose of the residence permit if the foreigner, with the approval of the Competent Authority, carries out certain administrative duties in a company in which he is a partner and authorized to sign on its behalf.

## Chapter Four: Recruitment of Expatriates (17-20)

### Article 17

The Official / Officer responsible for the expatriate’s residency is determined as follows:

1- The Employer alone, with respect to the expatriate Employee / Worker.

2- The head of the family, with respect to his family members residing with him in the State, and it is permissible for the woman’s residency to remain under the responsibility of the head of the family, even if she joins any work.

If the marital relationship ends for any reason, it is permissible for any family member to transfer to another sponsor after obtaining the approval of the Competent Authority, in accordance with the regulations issued by the minister.

3- The host, with respect to the expatriate visitor.

4- The relevant authorities in the State, with respect to expatriates for other purposes.

### Article 18

The importer, whether a natural or legal person, must meet the following conditions:

1- Must be Qatari, or an expatriate resident in the State in accordance with the Law / Act. If the importer is a legal person, its main headquarters must be in the State or have a branch office there.

2- Must be qualified to bear the responsibility imposed on him by this Law / Act towards the expatriate.

### Article 19

The recruiter is obligated to comply with the following: 1- Notify the Competent Authority within fourteen days from the date the expatriate leaves his employment, or refuses to leave the State after the cancellation of his residence permit or the expiration of the permit and the lapse of the period stipulated in Article (8/ second Paragraph) of this Law / Act, or the expiration of his visit period or the purpose for which he was granted entry. 2- Bearing the expenses of deporting the expatriate to his country in the cases stipulated in this Law / Act. If it is proven that the expatriate was working for another entity in violation of the provisions of this Law / Act, that entity shall bear the cost of his deportation. If it is impossible to identify such entity, the expatriate shall bear those expenses. In the event of his inability to pay and if he has no funds in the State, the recruiter shall bear those expenses. In all cases, anyone who employs any expatriate not recruited by him to work, in violation of the provisions of this Law / Act, shall pay for the expenses of deportation, without prejudice to any liability established by law. 3- Bearing the expenses of preparing and burying the body of a deceased expatriate in the cemeteries designated for that in the State, regardless of the cause of death. If one of the heirs of the deceased or any concerned party requests the transfer of the body outside the State, the recruiter shall bear the transportation costs to the expatriate’s original home or permanent place of residence.

### Article 20

The Ministry may require the importer of a foreign worker subject to the referenced Labor Law to provide a bank guarantee to ensure fulfillment of his obligations towards the Ministry and the worker, in accordance with the regulations issued by the Minister.

The Ministry may also, if the importer is a public employee and fails to fulfill his obligations to the worker, collect the costs of deportation from his salary and entitlements, in coordination with his employer.

## Chapter Five: Changing Employer (21-23)

### Article 21 Amended

The expatriate worker may transfer to work for another employer, in accordance with the rules and procedures set by the Ministry of Administrative Development, Labor and Social Affairs.

### Article 22 Canceled

The Minister or his delegate may approve the transfer of the expatriate worker to work temporarily for another employer, in the event of disputes between the expatriate worker and his recruiter, provided the approval of the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs is obtained, and with respect to the worker who is subject to the aforementioned Labour Law.

The Minister or his delegate may approve the transfer of the expatriate worker, who is not subject to the aforementioned Labour Law, to another employer in case the recruiter is proven to be abusive, or if it is required by the public interest.

For the same reasons, with the approval of the Minister or his delegate, based on the request of the expatriate worker and the approval of the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs, the transfer of the expatriate worker, who is subject to the aforementioned Labour Law, to another employer may be permitted.

### Article 23 Amended

No natural or legal person may employ expatriates without concluding an employment contract, or employ expatriates brought in to work for them to work for other entities.

Exceptionally, the Ministry of Administrative Development, Labor and Social Affairs may authorize the employer to second expatriate workers to another employer to work for them for a period not exceeding six months, renewable for a similar period.

It may also authorize the expatriate to work part-time for another employer in addition to their original job. In all cases, the approval of both the employer and the expatriate must be obtained for employment.

## Chapter Six: Departure, Deportation, Removal, and Return (24-28)

### Article 24

The expatriate shall leave the State if he does not obtain a License for residence In accordance with the provisions of this Law.

He must also leave the State if his residence License expires, is cancelled for any reason, or if the purpose for which the License was granted has ended, within ninety days from the date of expiration or cancellation of the License or the end of the purpose.

The expatriate may, after the approval of the Competent Authority, return to the State if he meets the necessary entry conditions, In accordance with the provisions of this Law and its executive regulations.

### Article 25

By way of exception to the provisions of any other law, the Minister may issue an order to deport any expatriate if it is proven that their presence in the State threatens its security or safety internally or externally, or harms the national economy or Public Health or public morals.

### Article 26

If the Employee / Worker is dismissed for disciplinary reasons, in accordance with the provisions of the Labour Law referred to, or pursuant to the provisions of laws regulating the affairs of State employees, or any other law, and does not appeal the Decision / Resolution before the competent Court, or appeals it and his appeal is rejected by a Final Judgment from the competent Court, he may not return to the State for work except after four years from the date of his departure.

The expatriate against whom a court judgment of deportation or removal has been issued may not return to the State except by a Decision / Resolution from the minister.

### Article 27

The minister, or his delegate, may grant the expatriate who has been ordered to be deported and who has interests in the State that require time to settle, a grace period not exceeding ninety days, renewable for one or more similar periods, provided that an acceptable guarantee is submitted.

### Article 28

The Minister may, when necessary, detain the expatriate against whom a Judgment / Ruling has been issued for deportation or an order for removal from the State in the designated place for thirty days, which may be renewed for another similar period.

The Minister may also, if it is not possible to execute the Judgment / Ruling or order mentioned in the previous paragraph, impose on the expatriate residence in a specific location for two weeks, which may be renewed.

The expatriate must report to the Department / Directorate of security within whose jurisdiction this location falls at the times specified in the order issued in this regard, until deportation or removal is carried out.

## Chapter Seven: Entry, Exit, and Residence of Certain Categories (29-37)

### Article 29

The entry, exit, and residence of the categories specified in this chapter shall be in accordance with the provisions stated herein.

### Article 30 Amended

The competent authority may issue entry visas and grant residence permits without a sponsor for the following categories:

1- Investors subject to the provisions of the Law / Act Regulating the investment of non-Qatari capital in economic activity.

2- Owners of real estate and beneficiaries thereof, in accordance with the provisions of the Law / Act Regulating the ownership and use of real estate by non-Qataris.

3- Any other categories determined by a decision of the Cabinet / Council of Ministers.

### Article 31

Licenses for residency for the categories mentioned in the previous article require the following:

1- The applicant must submit supporting documents for his request.

2- The applicant must have good conduct and reputation.

### Article 32 Amended

The residence period for property owners and beneficiaries shall be five years, automatically renewable upon payment of the prescribed fees, throughout the period of their ownership or benefit of the properties and in accordance with the regulations issued by the Cabinet / Council of Ministers.

The residence period for investors and other categories shall be five years, renewable for one or more similar periods.

### Article 33

Residence may be granted without the requirement of work to the spouse, children, and parents of the person licensed for residence, in accordance with the provision of Article (30) of this Law / Act.

### Article 34

The resident may leave the State during its validity period without obtaining permission or a Permit.

### Article 35

The license holder is prohibited from violating the purpose for which he was licensed to reside, except after obtaining permission to do so from the Competent Authority.

### Article 36 Amended

The Minister, or his delegate, may grant the licensee and his family members a grace period to leave the State not exceeding ninety days from the date of expiration of his residence, and this period may be extended if necessary.

### Article 37

The residence permit of the licensee shall be revoked in the following cases:

1- If it is proven that it was obtained based on false information or documents.

2- If the continuation of his residence poses a threat to the security or safety of the State at home or abroad, or harms the national economy, Public Health, or public morals.

3- If he violates or no longer fulfills the purpose for which he was granted the residence permit, without obtaining permission from the competent authorities.

## Chapter Eight: Penalties (38-41)

### Article 38

Without prejudice to any more severe penalty prescribed by another Law, the Penalty / Sentence shall be imprisonment for a period not exceeding three years and a fine not exceeding (50,000) fifty thousand Riyals, or one of these sentences, for anyone who violates any of the provisions of Articles (2/first Paragraph), (3), (4/third Paragraph), (11/second Paragraph), (16/first Paragraph), (23/first Paragraph), (24/first and second Paragraphs), (28/third Paragraph), (35) of this Law / Act.

The Penalty / Sentence in the case of recidivism shall be imprisonment for a period not less than thirty days and not exceeding three years, and a fine not less than (20,000) twenty thousand Riyals and not exceeding (100,000) one hundred thousand Riyals, or one of these sentences.

The accused shall be considered a recidivist if he commits a similar crime within one year from the date of completion of the execution of the prescribed Penalty / Sentence or its lapse by the passage of time.

### Article 39

Without prejudice to any more severe penalty prescribed by another Law:

1- A fine not exceeding (25,000) twenty-five thousand riyals shall be imposed on anyone who violates the provisions of Article (8/ Paragraph Three) of this Law / Act.

2- A fine not exceeding (50,000) fifty thousand riyals shall be imposed on anyone who violates the provisions of Article (19/ Item 1) of this Law / Act.

### Article 40

Without prejudice to any more severe penalty prescribed by another Law:

1- A fine not exceeding (20,000) twenty thousand riyals shall be imposed on anyone who violates the Ruling of Article (6) of this Law / Act.

2- A fine not exceeding (10,000) ten thousand riyals shall be imposed on anyone who violates any of the provisions of Articles (5/ Paragraph One), (8/Paragraph One and Two), (10/ Paragraph One), (13/ Paragraph One and Three), (15) of this Law / Act.

### Article 41

It is not permitted to suspend the execution of the Penalty of Fine adjudicated in one of the Crimes stipulated in this Law / Act.

## Chapter Nine: Conciliation (42-45)

### Article 42

The Minister or his delegate may approve the conduct of Conciliation in the Crimes specified in the Conciliation table attached to this Law / Act, prior to the issuance of a Final Judgment in the criminal Lawsuit / Legal Action, in exchange for the accused paying the amount specified in the table corresponding to the Crime attributed to him within the period determined by the Competent Authority.

If the accused rejects Conciliation or fails to execute it, the procedures of the criminal Lawsuit / Legal Action must proceed.

### Article 43

Employees of the Competent Authority undertake the Conciliation in the Crimes stipulated in this Law / Act, and the Conciliation is presented to the accused and recorded in the Report / Official Record. The accused who wishes to proceed with the Conciliation must pay the amount specified in the attached table to this Law / Act, in exchange for waiving the pursuit of the criminal Lawsuit / Legal Action, and the Conciliation amount shall be deposited in the Ministry's treasury.

The criminal Lawsuit / Legal Action is terminated by Conciliation, and Conciliation does not result in the forfeiture of the right to collect fees.

### Article 44

The accused of any of the Crimes stipulated in this Law / Act is not permitted to leave the State before paying the specified amount for settlement, the issuance of a Final Judgment of acquittal in the Lawsuit / Legal Action, or the execution of the Penalty / Sentence imposed upon him, as the case may be. However, he may be permitted to leave the State if he provides a guarantor who undertakes to pay the specified amount for settlement or the Fine ruled against him when it is due.

By way of exception to the provision of the previous paragraph, the minister may order the cancellation of the residence License of the expatriate accused of any of the Crimes stipulated in this Law / Act and order his deportation from the State, if he does not pay the specified amount for settlement, or if the Court sentences him to imprisonment or a Fine and he does not pay it.

### Article 45

The Minister, or whomever he delegates, may, in accordance with the requirements of public interest or for humanitarian considerations, exempt the accused from the reconciliation amount stipulated for any of the Crimes specified in this Law / Act, or reduce it to the extent he deems appropriate.

Tamim Bin Hamad Al-thani — Amir of the State of Qatar
