# Council of Ministers Decision No. (41) of 2019 Issuing the Executive Regulation of the Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Terrorism Financing Law Issued by Law No. (20) of 2019

Cabinet / Council of Ministers,

after reviewing the Constitution,

and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Law issued by Law No. (20) of 2019,

and Amiri Resolution No. (29) of 1996 regarding the Cabinet / Council of Ministers’ resolutions submitted to the Amir for ratification and issuance,

and based on the proposal of the Chairman of the National Committee for Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT),

has issued the following Decision / Resolution:

## الفصل الأول: تعاريف (1)

### Article 1

In the application of the provisions of these regulations, the following words and phrases shall have the meanings set out opposite each of them, unless the context otherwise requires:

Law / Act: Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Counter-Terrorist Financing Law issued by Law / Act No. (20) of 2019.

Politically Exposed Persons: Individuals entrusted, or who have been entrusted, with prominent public functions in the State or in a foreign State, such as heads of states or governments, senior politicians and government officials, judicial and military officials, senior executive officials in State-owned companies, members of legislative councils, key officials of political parties, as well as senior management members including directors, deputy directors, members of the Board of Directors, or positions equivalent thereto in international organizations.

Beneficial Owner: The natural person who ultimately owns or actually controls the client, whether through ownership shares or voting rights, or the natural person on whose behalf transactions are carried out, whether by agency, guardianship, trusteeship, or any other form of representation. This also includes the person who exercises effective and ultimate control over a legal person or legal arrangement, including anyone who exercises actual and final control by any means.

Correspondent Payment Accounts: Correspondent accounts that are used directly by third parties to conduct business on their own behalf.

Originating Financial Institution: The financial institution that initiates the wire transfer and transfers funds upon receiving a wire transfer request on behalf of the originator.

Beneficiary Financial Institution: The financial institution that receives the wire transfer directly from the originating financial institution or through an intermediary financial institution, and makes the funds available to the beneficiary.

Intermediary Financial Institution: The financial institution that, in the payment chain or cover, receives and transmits the wire transfer on behalf of the originating financial institution, the beneficiary financial institution, or another intermediary financial institution.

False Declaration: The provision of incorrect information about the value of currency, bearer negotiable instruments, metals, or precious stones being transported, or the provision of other incorrect relevant information required in the declaration or by customs authorities, including failure to provide the declaration as required.

Agent of a Money or Value Transfer Service Provider: Any person who provides money or value transfer services on behalf of a money or value transfer service provider, whether under contract or under their management.

## الفصل الثاني: الأنشطة والعمليات والتدابير الوقائية (2-40)

### الفرع الأول: أنشطة وعمليات المؤسسات المالية (2)

#### Article 2

The activities and operations carried out by the financial institution as a business include:

1- Receiving deposits and other funds payable from the public.

2- Lending, including consumer loans and mortgages, with or without recourse, and financing business operations, including the purchase of export documents, and the purchase of debts, with or without recourse.

3- Financial leasing, except for financial leasing related to consumer products.

4- Transfer of Funds or value services, excluding the provision of financial institutions with a message or other support systems for Transfer of Funds only.

5- Issuance or management of payment instruments, such as credit and debit cards, checks, traveler’s checks, money orders, bank checks, electronic money, payment orders, and bank drafts.

6- Financial guarantees and undertakings.

7- Activities related to Securities.

8- Trading in:

- Money Market instruments, such as checks, bills of exchange, certificates of deposit, and financial derivatives.

- Foreign Exchange Market.

- Currency exchange instruments.

- Interest Rates, and indices.

- Transferable Securities.

- Commodity futures contracts.

9- Participation in the issuance of Securities and the provision of financial services related to this issuance.

10- Individual or collective management of investment portfolios.

11- Safekeeping and management of funds or cash on behalf of or for the benefit of others.

12- Other operations for the investment, management, or operation of funds or cash on behalf of or for the benefit of others.

13- Subscription or savings in life insurance, and other types of insurance connected with investment, including those provided by insurance agents and intermediaries.

14- Exchanging money or currencies.

15- Any other activity or operation as determined by a decision from the Cabinet / Council of Ministers, based on a proposal from the Committee.

### الفرع الثاني: التدابير الوقائية (3-40)

#### Article 3

Financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions must identify, assess, and understand their risks of money laundering and terrorist financing, in a manner appropriate to the nature and size of their activities, and in accordance with the following:

1- Documenting Risk Assessments and any underlying information so that they are able to show their basis, monitor them, and update them regularly.

2- Making the Risk Assessment report available to the competent regulatory authority periodically within the period it determines, and upon request of this authority.

3- Taking into account all relevant risk factors before determining the risk mitigation measures to be applied and their type.

#### Article 4

Financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions must, when identifying risks in accordance with the previous article, take into account the risks identified in the national risk Assessment, in addition to the following factors:

1- Risk factors related to clients, the ultimate beneficiaries of clients, and the beneficiaries of transactions conducted by clients.

2- Risk factors related to countries and geographic regions.

3- Risk factors related to products, services provided by financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions, transactions, and delivery channels.

4- Risk factors related to the purpose of the client's account opening or establishment of the business relationship.

5- Risk factors related to the level of deposits and the volume of operations and transactions.

6- Risk factors related to the duration of the client relationship and the frequency of operations.

#### Article 5

Financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions must identify and assess the risks of money laundering and terrorist financing that may arise from the development of new products or professional practices, including new ways of delivering services or products, operations, or those resulting from the use of new or developing technologies applied to new or existing products. This must be done before launching, using, or adopting the products, practices, or technologies, and appropriate measures must be taken to manage and mitigate those risks.

#### Article 6

Financial institutions and Designated Non-Financial Businesses or Professions (DNFBPs) develop Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism financing programs that include the necessary policies, procedures, and controls, in a manner that takes into account risks and business size. These programs include the following:

1- Appropriate arrangements for compliance management, including the appointment of a compliance officer at the Department / Directorate level.

2- Taking appropriate screening measures to ensure high competence standards when hiring employees.

3- An ongoing training program for employees.

4- An independent audit unit to test the Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism financing system.

#### Article 7

Financial groups, businesses, and designated non-financial professions must implement Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism financing programs across all branches and subsidiaries in which the group holds a majority interest. In addition to the procedures stipulated in the previous article, these programs must include the following:

1- Implementing policies and procedures for the exchange of information required for customer due diligence purposes and for managing Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism financing risks.

2- Providing necessary information to compliance, audit, and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism financing officers at the group level about clients, accounts, and transactions from branches and subsidiaries when required for Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism financing purposes, including information and analysis regarding transactions and activities that appear unusual or suspicious, Suspicious Transaction Reporting (STR), their basic information, or any evidence of filing a suspicious transaction report.

3- Providing the information mentioned in the previous clause to branches and subsidiaries, when appropriate, whenever it is suitable for risk management.

4- Applying adequate safeguards regarding confidentiality and the use of exchanged information, including safeguards to prevent tipping-off.

#### Article 8

1- Financial groups, financial institutions, and designated non-financial businesses and professions must ensure that their foreign branches and majority-owned subsidiaries implement Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism financing measures in accordance with the requirements imposed in the State when the minimum Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism financing requirements in the host State are less stringent than those applied in the State, to the extent permitted by the laws and regulations of the host State.

2- If the host State does not allow the proper implementation of specific Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism financing measures consistent with the procedures applied by the State, the financial groups, financial institutions, and designated non-financial businesses and professions must apply appropriate additional procedures to manage the risks of money laundering and terrorism financing to their foreign branches and majority-owned subsidiaries, and report this to the competent supervisory authority in the State.

3- If the additional procedures are not sufficient, the competent authorities in the State must consider taking other supervisory measures, including imposing additional controls on financial groups, financial institutions, and designated non-financial businesses and professions, and requiring them, where appropriate, to cease their operations in the host State.

#### Article 9

Financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions must consider the relative importance and risks regarding their current customers when applying due diligence measures in the cases stipulated in Articles (10) and (11) of the Law / Act, and must take these measures towards existing business relationships in a timely manner, taking into account whether such measures have been taken previously, when they were taken, and the adequacy of the data obtained.

#### Article 10

Financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions must implement due diligence measures when conducting occasional financial transactions equal to or exceeding (50,000) fifty thousand riyals.

They must also take appropriate measures to detect operations split into smaller amounts that collectively equal the designated amount stated in the previous paragraph.

Real estate brokers apply these measures to both buyers and sellers of real estate.

#### Article 11

Dealers in Precious metals or Gemstones are subject to the obligations set forth in the Law / Act, when conducting cash transactions with their clients equal to or exceeding (50,000) fifty thousand riyals.

#### Article 12

Financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions must, when they suspect, during the establishment of a business relationship with a client or in the course of this relationship, or when conducting occasional transactions, that the operations are related to money laundering or Terrorism Financing, do the following:

1- Identify and verify the identity of the client and the beneficial owner, whether the client is permanent or occasional, regardless of any exemptions or applicable thresholds.

2- Submit a suspicious transaction report to the Unit.

#### Article 13

Financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions must identify and verify the identity of the customer using original documents or data from a trusted and independent source, by collecting at least the following information:

1- Regarding the natural person: obtaining the full name of the person as stated in the official proofs, the place of residence or local address, date and place of birth, and nationality.

2- Regarding the legal person or legal arrangement: obtaining the name of the person and its legal form, its founding document, the authorities and regulations governing the legal person or legal arrangement, the names of persons occupying relevant senior management functions, the address of the registered Office, and the main place of business if different from the address of the registered Office.

Financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions must, with respect to customers who are legal persons or legal arrangements, understand the customer's ownership and control structure and verify the identity of the beneficial owners in accordance with the provisions of articles (15) and (17) of the Regulation.

They must collect and verify any additional information, according to the degree of risk posed by a particular customer.

Financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions, with respect to all customers, must do the following:

1- Understand the nature of the customer's business or activity pattern.

2- Ensure that any person claiming to act on behalf of the customer is authorized to do so, with their identity determined and verified according to paragraphs (1) and (2) of the first section of this article.

3- Understand the purpose and nature of the business relationship, and when necessary obtain information related to this purpose.

4- Apply ongoing due diligence measures to the business relationship, including:

a- Reviewing transactions conducted throughout the duration of the relationship to ensure they are consistent with the institution's knowledge of the customers, their business patterns, the risks they present, and, where necessary, the source of funds.

b- Ensuring that the documents, data, or information obtained pursuant to due diligence measures is adequate and continually updated, by reviewing existing records, especially for high-risk categories of customers.

#### Article 14

Financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions, in cases where regulatory authorities allow the establishment of a business relationship before verifying the client's identity, must adopt Risk Management procedures regarding the circumstances under which he can benefit from the business relationship.

#### Article 15

Financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions must, in relation to clients who are legal persons, identify the beneficial owner and take reasonable measures to verify their identity using relevant information or data obtained from a reliable source, as follows:

1- Identifying the natural person or persons who ultimately hold a controlling ownership interest of no less than 20% of the shares of the legal person, or Voting rights therein, and taking reasonable measures to verify their identity.

2- In cases where the beneficial owner's identity cannot be determined, or if there is doubt that the natural person holding a controlling share is the beneficial owner according to the previous clause, or if no natural person exercises control through ownership share, the financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions must identify the natural person or persons who exercise actual or legal control or dominance, by any means, whether direct or indirect, over the legal person, legal arrangement, executive bodies, general assembly, operation of the legal person, or other instruments of control or dominance.

3- If no natural person is identified according to the previous two clauses, financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions must identify the natural person occupying a senior management position at the legal person and verify their identity.

Financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions shall not accept a client, execute a transaction, or continue a business relationship if unable to identify at least one natural person who meets the requirements set out in this Article. In such cases, they must terminate the business relationship for existing clients and submit a suspicious transaction report to the Unit.

#### Article 16

If the client, or the controlling shareholder, is a publicly listed company subject to disclosure requirements that ensure sufficient transparency in verifying the beneficial owner, or a subsidiary in which it holds a controlling stake, it is permissible not to identify or verify the identity of any shareholder or beneficial owner in those companies. Identity data may be obtained from publicly available records, from the client, or from any other reliable sources.

#### Article 17

Financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions must, with regard to clients who are trusts, identify the beneficial owner and take reasonable measures to verify their identity by identifying the settlor, trustee, protector (if any), beneficiaries or class of beneficiaries, and any other natural person who exercises ultimate and effective control over the trust directly or indirectly.

With regard to other legal arrangements, they must identify the natural persons who occupy similar positions.

Financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions must also take necessary measures to determine whether the client is acting as a trustee of a trust or holds an equivalent or similar position in another type of legal arrangement.

#### Article 18

In addition to customer due diligence measures outlined in the Law / Act and the regulation, financial institutions apply the following additional due diligence measures regarding beneficiaries of life insurance documents and other investment insurance products, once those beneficiaries are identified or designated:

1- With regard to beneficiaries identified by name: The name of the person must be obtained, whether a natural person, a legal person, or a legal arrangement.

2- With regard to beneficiaries designated by characteristics, category, or other means: Sufficient information about the beneficiary must be obtained to enable the financial institution to be satisfied that it will be able to identify the beneficiary at the time of payout.

3- Verification of the beneficiary’s identity in both previous cases at the time of payout.

In cases where financial institutions are unable to carry out the measures referred to in this article, they must submit a suspicion report to the unit.

#### Article 19

Financial institutions must consider the beneficiary of a life insurance policy as a risk factor when determining the applicability of enhanced due diligence measures regarding customers.

If they determine that the beneficiary is a legal entity or arrangement and represents high risks, they must take enhanced due diligence measures, which include reasonable procedures to identify and verify the ultimate beneficial owner of the beneficiary of the policy at the time of payout.

#### Article 20

Financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions, when relying on third parties from financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions to carry out the due diligence measures stipulated in the Law / Act and the regulation, must take the following actions:

1- Obtain immediately the necessary information related to the aforementioned measures.

2- Ensure that the third party will provide without delay copies of customer identification data, and other documents related to those measures upon request.

3- Verify that the third party is subject to regulation and supervision or oversight, and is committed to customer due diligence measures and record keeping in accordance with the Law / Act and the regulation.

4- Consider the available information about the level of money laundering and terrorist financing risks in the countries where the third parties they rely on are located.

#### Article 21

When financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions rely on a third party that is part of the same group, the supervisory authorities, whether in the State or the host State, may consider that the requirements referred to in the previous article have been met in the following cases:

1- The group applies customer due diligence measures, record keeping, and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism financing programs, in accordance with the law and regulations.

2- Supervision is exercised by the competent authorities to verify the group’s implementation of these measures and programs.

3- High risks associated with countries are adequately mitigated through the group’s Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism financing policies.

#### Article 22

Financial institutions and Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions must apply enhanced due diligence measures proportionate to the level of risk to business relationships and transactions conducted with customers, including financial institutions and Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions from countries for which the Financial Action Task Force calls for such action, and which are published by the Committee on its website on the World Wide Web.

#### Article 23

Financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions must take additional measures in accordance with Article (13) of the Law / Act, including countermeasures proportional to the degree of risk identified in the circulars issued by regulatory authorities, based on data from the Financial Action Task Force, or as decided independently by the Committee.

#### Article 24

The Committee issues circulars related to weaknesses in Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and terrorism financing systems in other countries.

The Committee notifies the regulatory bodies and the competent authorities about these circulars and publishes them on its website on the international information network.

The regulatory bodies notify the financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions under their supervision accordingly.

#### Article 25

Financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions must, to the fullest extent possible and reasonably, examine the background and purpose of all complex or unusual transactions, as well as all patterns of unusual transactions that do not have a clear economic or legal purpose.

When risks of money laundering or Terrorism Financing are high, financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions must apply enhanced due diligence procedures, consistent with the identified risks. In particular, they must increase the degree of monitoring of the business relationship to identify unusual or suspicious activities or transactions. Enhanced due diligence procedures specifically include the following:

1- Obtaining additional information about the client, including occupation, asset size, information available from public databases and open sources, and regularly updating identification data for the client and beneficial owner.

2- Obtaining additional information about the expected nature of the business relationship.

3- Obtaining information on the source of funds or the source of the client's wealth.

4- Obtaining information on the reasons for expected or executed transactions.

5- Obtaining approval from senior management to initiate or continue the business relationship.

6- Applying enhanced monitoring of the business relationship by increasing the number and frequency of its supervision and selecting transaction patterns that require further examination and review.

7- Conducting the first payment through an account in the client's name at a bank subject to similar due diligence standards.

#### Article 26

If the risks of money laundering or Terrorism Financing are low, financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions, as specified by the regulatory authority, may apply simplified due diligence measures that take into account the nature of these risks and are proportionate to low-risk factors, including the following:

1- Verifying the identity of the client and the beneficial owner after establishing the business relationship, such as account operations if the amount exceeds the threshold set for the business relationship.

2- Reducing the frequency of client identity updates.

3- Lowering the level of ongoing due diligence and transaction scrutiny, based on a reasonable specified amount.

4- Not collecting information or carrying out specific procedures to understand the nature or purpose of the business relationship, relying instead on inference from the types of transactions conducted or from the existing business relationship.

Simplified measures may relate only to client acceptance or ongoing monitoring, and these measures must not be applied in cases of suspicion of money laundering or Terrorism Financing, or when specific high-risk situations are identified.

#### Article 27

Financial institutions, businesses, and designated non-financial professions must establish appropriate systems for risk management in a manner that enables the identification of whether the client or their beneficial owner is a politically exposed person, a family member, or a close associate. They must also take the following additional due diligence measures towards them:

1- Obtain the approval of senior management before establishing a business relationship or continuing it, in the case of existing clients.

2- Take reasonable measures to determine the source of wealth and funds of clients and beneficial owners who are politically exposed persons, or their family members or close associates.

3- Apply enhanced and ongoing monitoring of their business relationship.

#### Article 28

The family members of the politically exposed person include any natural person related to him by blood or marriage up to the second degree.

The close associate of the politically exposed person includes any natural person who is a partner with him in a legal entity or legal arrangement, or a beneficial owner of a legal entity or legal arrangement owned or actually controlled by a politically exposed person, or any person who has a close professional or social relationship with him.

#### Article 29

Financial institutions must take reasonable measures before disbursing entitlements, regarding life insurance documents, to determine whether the beneficiary or the ultimate beneficiary of the beneficiary are politically exposed persons.

They must also, in cases of high risk, notify the Department / Directorate senior management before disbursing the entitlements, conduct a thorough examination of the business relationship with the holder of this document, and submit a report of suspicious transactions to the Unit.

#### Article 30

Financial institutions, when establishing a cross-border correspondent banking relationship or any similar relationships, must do the following:

1- Collect sufficient information about the respondent institution to fully understand the nature of its business, and, through publicly available information, identify its reputation and the level of supervision it is subject to, including whether it has been subject to an Anti-Money Laundering (AML) or Terrorism Financing investigation or regulatory action.

2- Assess the controls used by the respondent institution for Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Terrorism Financing.

3- Obtain approval from senior management before establishing a new correspondent banking relationship.

4- Clearly understand the responsibilities of each institution in the field of Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Terrorism Financing.

#### Article 31

Regarding correspondent payment accounts, financial institutions must be satisfied that the respondent bank:

1- Has performed the due diligence measures stipulated in the Law / Act and the regulations concerning customers who can directly access its accounts.

2- Is able to provide relevant due diligence information about customers upon the correspondent bank’s request.

#### Article 32

Financial institutions issuing wire transfers must do the following:

1- Obtain information relating to the originator and beneficiary when conducting wire transfers equal to or exceeding (3,500) three thousand five hundred riyals, verify it, and ensure that such information includes the following:

a- The full name of the originator and beneficiary.

b- The originator's and beneficiary's account Number, or the unique transaction identification Number when there is no account Number, provided that this Number allows financial institutions to trace the transaction.

c- The originator's address or national identification Number or client identification Number or date and place of birth, and this information shall be included in the message or payment form accompanying the transfer.

2- In cases where the information referred to in the previous paragraph is available to the beneficiary financial institution and competent authorities through other means, the financial institution may be satisfied to include only the account Number or the unique transaction identification Number in the domestic wire transfer message, provided that these two numbers allow tracing the path of the transaction to the transfer originator or beneficiary, within three business days from the date of receiving the request, whether from the beneficiary financial institution or from competent authorities. The Public Prosecutor may require immediate provision of this information.

3- Ensure that cross-border wire transfers that are less than the amount specified in paragraph (1) of this article include the name of the originator and beneficiary, and the account Number of each or the unique transaction identification Number that allows tracking, and in this case the issuing financial institution is not required to verify the accuracy of the information except in cases of suspicion of money laundering or Terrorism Financing.

4- Ensure, in cases where several cross-border wire transfers issued by a single originator are consolidated in a batch transfer file for delivery to beneficiaries, that the file contains required and accurate information about the transfer originator and complete information about the beneficiary, enabling full traceability of these transactions within the country of the beneficiary financial institution, and include the originator's account Number or the unique transaction identification Number.

5- Refrain from executing wire transfers if the requirements of this article are not met.

#### Article 33

When conducting cross-border wire transfers, intermediary financial institutions must do the following:

1- Ensure that all information relating to the originator and beneficiary accompanies the wire transfer.

2- Take reasonable measures consistent with straight-through processing to identify wire transfers that do not include the required information about the originator or beneficiary.

#### Article 34

The beneficiary financial institution must take reasonable measures to identify cross-border wire transfers that lack the required information about the originator or beneficiary, which may include follow-up procedures after execution, or follow-up procedures at the time of execution, whenever possible.

For cross-border wire transfers exceeding the value of (3,500) three thousand five hundred riyals, the beneficiary financial institution must verify the identity of the beneficiary if this has not been done previously, and must retain the information collected during the verification process, in accordance with the requirements stipulated in the Law / Act.

#### Article 35

Financial institutions that are issuers, intermediaries, and beneficiaries, when conducting wire transfers, must retain all information related to the originator and beneficiary, including the originator's account number or a unique identifier for the transaction, for at least ten years from the date of completion of any transaction.

If technical restrictions prevent the retention of the required information about the originator and beneficiary accompanying a cross-border wire transfer along with the related domestic wire transfer, the intermediary financial institution must retain all information received from the issuing financial institution or from another intermediary financial institution for at least ten years.

#### Article 36

Intermediary and beneficiary financial institutions must establish effective risk-based policies and procedures to determine when to execute, reject, or suspend wire transfers that do not include the required information about the originator or beneficiary, as well as appropriate follow-up procedures.

#### Article 37

Money or value transfer service providers must:

1- Comply with all relevant requirements in the Law / Act and the regulation, whether they conduct their activities themselves or through their agents.

2- Maintain an updated list of their agents and make it available to the relevant supervisory authority.

3- Include their agents in their Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism financing programs and monitor their compliance with them.

#### Article 38

If the provider of the Transfer of Funds or value service controls both parties to the transaction, the sender and the beneficiary, they must adhere to the following:

1- Take into consideration all information issued by the source of the Transfer of Funds and the beneficiary to determine whether it is necessary to file a suspicious transaction report.

2- Submit a report on suspicious wire transfers in all connected countries, and provide all relevant information regarding the transaction to the Unit.

#### Article 39

Financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions must submit Suspicious Transaction Reporting (STR), in accordance with the form approved by the Unit, and the instructions and guidelines it issues.

#### Article 40

It is not considered disclosure as stipulated in Article (22/ second Paragraph) of the Law / Act, the efforts made by lawyers, authorized notaries, accountants, and legal accountants to dissuade the client from committing an unlawful act.

## الفصل الثالث: الإقرار الجمركي (41-48)

### Article 41

Every natural person entering or leaving the territory of the State, in possession of currencies, negotiable financial instruments to the bearer, precious metals, or precious stones, or arranging for their transfer into or out of the State by another person, shipping, mail, or by any other means, must declare their value to the competent Customs officer if their value is equal to or exceeds (50,000) fifty thousand riyals, and fill out the form prepared for this purpose by the Customs authorities.

### Article 42

When a legal entity imports or exports a shipment of currencies, negotiable financial instruments, precious metals, or gemstones, if its value is equal to or exceeds fifty thousand (50,000) riyals, it must declare its value, fill out the form prepared for this purpose by the customs authorities, and obtain the necessary approvals from the competent authorities.

### Article 43

All non-profit organizations wishing to transfer bearer negotiable instruments, currencies, precious metals, or gemstones, if their value is equal to or exceeds (50,000) fifty thousand riyals, must declare their value and fill out the form prepared for this purpose by the customs authorities, after submitting evidence of obtaining the Authority's approval in accordance with the provisions of the Law / Act Regulating charitable activities.

### Article 44

The competent Customs officer, in the event of failure to declare or the submission of a false declaration regarding currencies or bearer negotiable instruments, or Precious metals or Gemstones, with a value equal to or exceeding fifty thousand (50,000) riyals, shall take the following actions:

1- Seize the currencies or bearer negotiable instruments, or Precious metals or Gemstones.

2- Prepare an Official Report of the incident.

3- Request additional information from the violator regarding the source of the currencies or bearer negotiable instruments, or Precious metals or Gemstones, and the purpose of transferring them.

The Customs officer may also detain the persons involved in the incident of transferring currencies or bearer negotiable instruments, or Precious metals or Gemstones, and immediately hand them over to the competent security department of the Ministry of Interior. The Official Report and seized items shall be referred to the Public Prosecution for further action.

### Article 45

The competent Customs officer, if the importer, exporter, legal entity, or non-profit organization fails to submit a declaration regarding a shipment of currencies or bearer negotiable instruments, or Precious metals or Gemstones, whose value equals or exceeds (50,000) fifty thousand riyals, or if a false declaration is submitted regarding them, shall take the following actions:

1- Seizing the shipment of currencies or bearer negotiable instruments, or Precious metals or Gemstones.

2- Preparing a Report / Official Record of the incident.

3- Requesting additional information from the importer, exporter, legal entity, non-profit organization, or the Legal Representative of any of them, regarding the reason for not submitting the declaration or submitting a false declaration.

The Customs officer may also detain persons involved in the incident of transporting currencies or bearer negotiable instruments, or Precious metals or Gemstones, and hand them over to the competent security department at the Ministry of Interior. The Report / Official Record and the seized items shall be referred to the Public Prosecution to take the necessary procedures.

### Article 46

The customs authorities must inform the Unit of suspected cases related to the transportation of currencies or bearer negotiable financial instruments, or Precious metals or Gemstones, in connection with money laundering crimes, predicate crimes, or terrorism financing crimes, or when a false declaration is made, or when the bearer fails to submit a declaration.

### Article 47

The Customs authorities collect data and information regarding the movement of currencies or bearer negotiable financial instruments, or Precious metals or Gemstones, and may, for this purpose, exercise the following powers:

1- Gathering declaration forms regarding the value of currencies or bearer negotiable financial instruments, or Precious metals or Gemstones, held by persons arriving in or departing from the State.

2- Verifying the accuracy of the information contained in the declaration forms.

3- Ensuring that currencies or bearer negotiable financial instruments are free from counterfeiting.

4- Ensuring that currencies are still valid according to their issuance laws.

5- If Precious metals or Gemstones held by persons arriving in or departing from the State are for commercial purposes, Customs authorities must verify their value based on the purchase invoice.

6- Entry of the information contained in the declaration forms by Customs personnel into a database and making it available to the unit.

7- Retaining documents and data related to currencies or bearer negotiable financial instruments, or Precious metals or Gemstones whose values have been declared or disclosed, and the identity of their holders, for at least ten years in the following cases:

a- Declarations whose value equals or exceeds (50,000) fifty thousand riyals.

b- False declarations.

c- Suspicion of money laundering or terrorism financing.

8- Retaining statistics and reporting the volume of currencies or bearer negotiable financial instruments, or Precious metals or Gemstones, entering or leaving the State, false declarations, actions taken by Customs authorities, and any other statistics requested by the Committee.

9- Information Exchange regarding the value of currencies or bearer negotiable financial instruments, or Precious metals or Gemstones whose values have been declared or disclosed, and the identities of their holders with relevant local authorities, and may exchange this information with relevant foreign authorities based on the principle of reciprocity or in accordance with International Agreements.

10- Cooperation and coordination with relevant authorities for the purposes of implementing this Article.

### Article 48

The customs authorities issue decisions, instructions, and directives for the purposes of implementing the provisions of Chapter Four of the Law / Act.

## الفصل الرابع: اللجنة الوطنية لمكافحة غسل الأموال وتمويل الإرهاب (49-51)

### Article 49

The Committee establishes a database to record information received from various entities in the State regarding matters related to its field of work. This database includes statistics related to combating money laundering and terrorist financing, including reports of suspicious transactions received and referred, investigations and prosecutions, Conviction sentences related to money laundering and terrorist financing, frozen, seized, and confiscated assets, requests for mutual legal assistance, and other requests for International Cooperation.

### Article 50

The Committee is responsible for the following:

1- Verifying that the national risk Assessment covers all relevant sectors, including financial institutions, designated non-financial businesses and professions, non-profit organizations, cross-border movement of cash, in addition to the activities of law enforcement agencies, and the application of a risk-based approach to allocate resources and necessary measures to prevent or mitigate money laundering or Terrorism Financing.

2- Referring the national risk Assessment report to the Governor, to be submitted to the Cabinet / Council of Ministers for approval, and circulating its results to national entities concerned with Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Terrorism Financing, ensuring that these entities include the results within their work plans and implement them.

3- Overseeing the update of the national risk Assessment at least once every three years, and whenever necessary.

4- Coordinating with supervisory authorities to ensure that they inform the entities under their supervision of the results of the national risk Assessment and address the risks, according to the national strategy for Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Terrorism Financing and the financing of the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction.

5- Coordinating with the competent authorities to raise awareness of the risks of money laundering and Terrorism Financing, as well as coordinating with supervisory authorities to ensure the ability of financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions to combat money laundering, predicate offences, and Terrorism Financing.

### Article 51

The Committee works with regulatory bodies to identify and assess the risks of money laundering and terrorist financing that arise from the development of new products and new business practices, including the use of delivery channels, or the development of new technologies for modern or pre-existing products.

## الفصل الخامس: وحدة المعلومات المالية (52-58)

### Article 52

The unit has the authority to establish and approve the forms and procedures used for requesting and referring information and reporting reports, as well as other related reports and their associated timeframes, in coordination with the competent authorities, in order to access financial and administrative information and Law / Act enforcement information held by the competent authorities in the State, as well as relevant information collected or obtained by other authorities, or collected or obtained on their behalf.

The unit may also use publicly available information and rented databases.

### Article 53

The Unit takes the necessary measures to protect the information in its databases, or that it has the right to access or obtain, provided that these measures are at least consistent with the standards set by the Egmont Group for information exchange between financial intelligence units, and any international standards currently or future applied in the State. To this end, it must do the following:

1- Issuing rules that govern the security, confidentiality, and Privacy of information at all stages of its processing, transfer, protection, and access.

2- Ensuring that the Unit’s employees obtain the necessary Security Clearances, and comprehend their responsibilities regarding handling the information, especially that which concerns Privacy and confidentiality, and its transfer.

3- Ensuring the application of a restricted and limited access system by the Unit to its facilities and information, including its information technology systems.

4- Using designated, safe, and protected channels to transfer information to the competent authorities inside and outside the State.

### Article 53 (Repeated)

The head of the Unit shall have the necessary powers to manage the affairs of the Unit and achieve its objectives, in particular the following:

1- Preparing the Unit's plans, programs, and projects, including training and employment, and following up on their implementation.

2- Issuing the Organizational Structure of the Unit.

3- Approving the Unit's annual estimated budget and its final accounts.

4- Proposing the approval of draft Agreements and memoranda of understanding in which the Unit is a party; these drafts shall be submitted to the Cabinet / Council of Ministers for the appropriate Decision / Resolution.

The Decisions / Resolutions of the head of the Unit stipulated in items (2) and (3) shall not be effective except after being approved by the Governor.

### Article 54

The Unit records in its database the information available to it about suspected operations, the individuals suspected of conducting them, and information related to money laundering, predicate Crimes, and Terrorism Financing.

The competent authorities are obliged to inform the Unit of any information they have regarding money laundering Crimes, predicate Crimes, or Terrorism Financing Crimes, as well as the actions taken regarding them and the outcomes thereof, whenever these actions and results are related to measures required or approved by the Unit.

### Article 55

Based on what the Unit receives from suspicious transaction reports from financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions, or information related to money laundering or predicate Crimes or Terrorism Financing, it shall, in particular, do the following:

1- Operational analysis: using available information that can be obtained to identify specific targets, track the course of certain activities and transactions, and identify links between these targets, and any proceeds that may be related to money laundering Crimes, predicate Crimes, or Terrorism Financing Crimes.

Strategic analysis: using available information and information that can be obtained, including data that may be provided by other competent authorities, to identify trends and patterns of money laundering and Terrorism Financing.

### Article 56

The Unit must do the following:

1- Determine the requirements for the collection and analysis of information from financial institutions and Designated Non-Financial Businesses or Professions (DNFBPs).

2- Approve reporting requirements from financial institutions and Designated Non-Financial Businesses or Professions (DNFBPs), as well as the necessary forms and procedures, in coordination with regulatory agencies and competent authorities.

3- Approve the forms and procedures for requesting information from the entities obligated to report, in coordination with regulatory agencies.

4- Notify regulatory agencies in cases where a financial institution or any of the Designated Non-Financial Businesses or Professions (DNFBPs) does not comply with the requirements stipulated in this article.

### Article 57

The Unit engages in Information Exchange and cooperation with the competent authorities and supervisory entities, as follows:

1- Automatically referring any information and results of its analyses when it has reasons to believe that this information is useful in identifying proceeds of a crime or Terrorism Financing, and the Unit exclusively determines this information.

2- Considering the provision of any information it holds based on its discretionary authority upon request.

3- Establishing mechanisms and procedures whereby competent authorities can request information and responding to urgent information requests.

### Article 58

The Unit keeps statistics on received suspicious transaction reports and referred reports, on topics of International Cooperation, and any other topics requested by the Committee.

## الفصل السادس: الجهات الرقابية (59-65)

The regulatory authorities also include any other authority that is legally competent to regulate, supervise, or provide Financial Institutions Oversight over financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions, or non-profit organizations.

### Article 60

Regulatory authorities shall have the following powers:

1- Monitoring and inspection of financial institutions, designated non-financial businesses and professions, and non-profit organizations, to ensure their compliance with the requirements of the Law / Act, the Regulation, and any instructions, rules, or others issued by them. For this purpose, they may obtain any information they request in accordance with the provisions of Article (41) of the Law / Act.

2- Issuing instructions, rules, guidelines, recommendations, or any other tools, to implement the provisions of the Law / Act and the Regulation or any other provisions, for the purpose of Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Terrorism Financing, as well as setting guidelines and providing feedback to supervised entities, regarding compliance with the requirements stipulated in the Law / Act and the Regulation, and instruments issued by the regulatory authorities to enhance the effectiveness of applicable policies and procedures.

3- Assisting the Unit in establishing procedures necessary for reporting suspicious transactions, in line with relevant national and international standards. Regulatory authorities are obliged to notify the Unit and the Public Prosecutor if, during the exercise of their powers, they discover information that may be related to money laundering crimes, predicate offenses, or Terrorism Financing crimes.

4- Cooperation and exchange of information related to the detection and reporting of money laundering crimes, predicate offenses, and Terrorism Financing crimes with competent authorities.

### Article 61

When exercising their authorities and powers in Financial Institutions Oversight and oversight of designated non-financial businesses and professions and non-profit organizations, regulatory bodies must use a risk-based approach to ensure the implementation of Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Terrorism Financing measures, and to determine the degree and focus of oversight. In particular, they must do the following:

1- Verify that the entities under their oversight have systems that enable them to meet their obligations for Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Terrorism Financing as stipulated by the Law / Act, and follow up on their implementation.

2- Ensure that the entities under their oversight, as well as their foreign branches and majority-owned subsidiaries abroad, apply Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Terrorism Financing measures in accordance with the requirements imposed in the State when the minimum requirements for Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Terrorism Financing in the host State are less stringent than those applied in the State, to the extent permitted by the laws and regulations of the host State.

When exercising their authorities and prescribed powers over financial institutions in accordance with the Law / Act and regulations, regulatory bodies must do the following:

1- With regard to financial institutions subject to the core principles, ensure the extent of their regulation and supervision in line with these principles where related to Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Terrorism Financing, including the application of consolidated supervision at the financial group level for Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Terrorism Financing purposes.

2- With regard to other financial institutions, ensure the extent of their regulation, supervision, or monitoring, in accordance with the level of Anti-Money Laundering (AML) or Terrorism Financing risk in this sector.

### Article 62

Regulatory authorities determine the frequency and level of supervision for the purposes of Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and combating the financing of terrorism, based on the following:

1- The risks of money laundering and terrorist financing and their understanding of them, as well as the internal controls, policies, and procedures implemented by the institution or financial group and their adequacy, as determined by the competent regulatory authority when assessing the risk characteristics of this institution or group.

2- The risks of money laundering and terrorist financing present in the State.

3- The diversity and number of financial institutions, designated non-financial businesses and professions, and non-profit organizations, and the degree of discretion granted to them under the risk-based approach.

### Article 63

Regulatory authorities must do the following:

1- Ensure that financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions conduct their Risk Assessment, review the characteristics of money laundering and terrorist financing risks, and analyze the results of these reviews to identify the challenges they face in complying with Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Counter-Terrorist Financing standards.

2- They must periodically review the Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Counter-Terrorist Financing Risk Assessment related to financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions, and whenever there are any significant changes in their management or operations.

2- Provide suggestions on how to enhance the effectiveness and efficiency of the procedures and policies followed.

3- Use the results of the Risk Assessment to assign supervisors, guide their supervision, and determine the method and degree of Inspection.

They must also keep statistics on the measures adopted and sanctions imposed under the Law / Act, international cooperation, and any other subjects determined by the Committee, and provide these statistics to it.

### Article 64

The Raising a Grievance provided for in Article (44) of the Law / Act shall be submitted by the concerned party to the competent regulatory authority within fifteen days from the date of being notified in writing of the issued Decision / Resolution concerning him, or from the date he became aware of it. The Raising a Grievance must include the following information:

1- The name, surname, capacity, and address of the complainant.

2- The Decision / Resolution being challenged, its date of issuance, and the date the complainant was notified of the Decision / Resolution or became aware of it.

3- The reasons upon which the Raising a Grievance is based, the supporting documents, and the explanatory memoranda.

4- The specific requests of the complainant.

5- The appropriate means for the complainant to receive notifications related to the consideration of the Raising a Grievance, whether via fax, e-mail, telephone, or other means.

### Article 65

The competent regulatory authority shall notify the complainant of the date set for considering his Raising a Grievance and of all related papers and documents, using the notification method specified in the previous article.

If the complainant does not attend in person or through an agent on the date scheduled for considering the Raising a Grievance, he shall be notified of another date within seven days; if he still does not attend, the grievance shall be decided in his absence.

In all cases, the competent regulatory authority must make a decision on the Raising a Grievance within a period not exceeding thirty days from the date of its submission.

The Decision / Resolution must include a summary of the subject of the Raising a Grievance and the reasons on which it was based. The complainant shall be notified in writing with a copy of the Decision / Resolution within seven days from the date of its issuance, using the notification method specified in his Raising a Grievance.

## الفصل السابع: التعاون الدولي (66-79)

### الفرع الأول: المساعدة القانونية المتبادلة (66-67)

#### Article 66

When the request for mutual legal assistance pertains to issuing and executing a confiscation order, the competent Court shall consider it based on a request from the Attorney General, in accordance with the provisions of Article (68) of the Law / Act.

#### Article 67

Subject to the provisions of Bilateral Agreements or multilateral Agreements to which the State is a party, or any arrangements or memoranda of understanding regarding the sharing of confiscated assets with foreign countries, the Attorney General may decide to deduct a Part of the confiscated funds.

### الفرع الثاني: التعاون بين الوحدة ونظيراتها الأجنبية (68-69)

#### Article 68

The unit may cooperate to the maximum extent possible with its foreign counterparts either automatically or upon their request, in accordance with the rules determined by Bilateral Agreements or multilateral agreements to which it is a party, including cooperation with counterpart units within the framework of the [Egmont] Group, or based on arrangements or memoranda of understanding concluded with them, or according to the principle of reciprocity, regardless of the nature and status of its counterparts.

#### Article 69

The Unit may request information from foreign counterpart Units, and in this case, the Unit must provide all relevant information available to it, including a description of the case under analysis and the possibility of its connection to the country receiving the request for information.

The Unit may exchange with its foreign counterparts all information it has received or collected, in accordance with the provisions of Chapter Six of the Law / Act, and any other information that the Unit may obtain or access directly or indirectly from national sources.

The Unit must provide feedback to its foreign counterpart upon request regarding the use of the information it has provided and the results of analyses conducted based on that information.

### الفرع الثالث: التعاون بين الجهات الرقابية على المؤسسات المالية ونظيراتها الأجنبية (70-74)

#### Article 70

Regulatory authorities over financial institutions must cooperate to the greatest extent possible in the field of supervision, including the exchange of supervisory information for the purposes of Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and combating the financing of terrorism, with their foreign counterparts automatically or upon request, in accordance with the rules established by Bilateral Agreements or multilateral agreements to which they are a party, or based on arrangements or memoranda of understanding concluded with them, or according to the principle of reciprocity, regardless of the nature and status of their counterparts, and in accordance with the applicable International Standards for such supervision.

#### Article 71

Regulatory authorities overseeing financial institutions may exchange all information available to them nationally with their foreign counterparts, including information held by the financial institutions under their supervision, in accordance with their specific needs.

#### Article 72

Regulatory authorities for financial institutions may exchange information with their foreign counterparts, especially those sharing joint responsibility for financial institutions belonging to the same financial group, for the purposes of Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and combating terrorism financing, including:

1- The national legal and regulatory framework and general information about financial sectors.

2- Prudential information, such as information related to the activities and operations of financial institutions, the beneficial owner, management, and competency and suitability standards.

3- Information related to Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and combating terrorism financing, such as internal procedures and policies of financial institutions, customer due diligence information and customer files, and templates of information related to accounts and financial transactions.

#### Article 73

Regulatory authorities over financial institutions may collect necessary information on behalf of their foreign counterparts and, when appropriate, assist them in obtaining such information themselves in order to facilitate effective supervision of financial institutions belonging to the same financial group.

#### Article 74 Amended

When regulatory authorities overseeing financial institutions provide information requested by their foreign counterparts, they must obtain prior authorization from them to refer that information to any entity or to use it for regulatory or non-regulatory purposes.

When the regulatory authority overseeing financial institutions is subject to a legal obligation to disclose or report such information, it must notify its foreign counterpart immediately upon receiving it.

### الفرع الرابع: التعاون بين جهات إنفاذ القانون ونظيراتها الأجنبية (75-77)

#### Article 75

Law enforcement authorities may exchange the information available to them with their foreign counterparts for the purposes of conducting Investigations and gathering evidence related to combating money laundering Crimes, predicate Crimes, and Terrorism Financing Crimes, including information related to identifying or tracing the proceeds or means of the Crime.

#### Article 76

Law enforcement agencies may use the powers granted to them under the Law / Act and the regulation, the Code of Criminal Procedure, and the special laws that govern their work, to conduct investigations on behalf of counterpart agencies or collect information requested by them, and exchange it with them.

In this regard, law enforcement agencies are subject to the requirements set out in Chapter Ten of the Law / Act.

#### Article 77

The provisions of the two previous articles apply to all competent authorities that have Judicial Police powers and conduct Investigations and inquiries to collect evidence for the purposes of Anti-Money Laundering (AML), combating the financing of terrorism, and predicate offenses. This includes specialized departments within the Ministry of Interior, the State Security Agency, and the General Customs Authority.

### الفرع الخامس: أشكال التعاون الأخرى (78-79)

#### Article 78

The competent authorities must use effective means to respond to information requests promptly.

In the event of responding to an information request, this must be done in accordance with the provisions of the applicable legislation, after verifying the purpose of the request, the entity on whose behalf the request was submitted, and, when necessary, verifying the authorization granted to the requesting السلطة.

#### Article 79

The competent authority may exchange information with a non-counterpart foreign competent authority indirectly, provided that the purpose of the information request and the entity submitting the request on its behalf are specified.

Information Exchange is considered indirect when the requested information is sent by the authority from which the information is requested, through a competent authority of the State or a competent authority of one or more foreign states, before being received by the requesting authority, taking into account obtaining an assignment or Authorization / Delegation that allows Information Exchange in this manner.
