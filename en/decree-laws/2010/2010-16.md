# Decree Law No 16 of 2010 Promulgating the Electronic Transactions and Commerce Law

We, Tamim Bin Hamad Al-Thani, Deputy Emir of the State of Qatar,

Having perused the Constitution,

Law No. 13 of 1990 concerning the Civil and Commercial Procedure Law and amending laws thereof,

The Civil Law No. 22 of 2004

Decree Law No. 36 of 2004 establishing the Supreme Council of Information and Communication Technology,;

The Trade Regulation Law No. 27 of 2006 as amended by Law No. 7 of 2010;

The Telecommunications Law promulgated by Decree Law No. 34 of 2006;

Law No. 8 of 2008 concerning Consumer Protection;

The proposal of the Supreme Council of Information and Communication Technology, and

The draft Law submitted by the Council of Ministers,

Hereby promulgate the following Law:

## Chapter One (1)

### Definitions (1)

#### Article 1

For the application of the provisions of this Law, the following words and phrases shall have the meanings indicated beside each, unless the context requires otherwise:

Supreme Council: Supreme Council for Telecommunications and Information Technology.

Person: Natural or legal person.

Electronic: The technique of using electrical, electromagnetic, optical, or any other similar means of technology.

Electronic communication: Communication made by any means of Telecommunications, whether wired or wireless.

Automated messaging system: A computer system, or any electronic communication means, or any other automated means, used to initiate or respond wholly or partially to electronic communications or to related actions, without review or intervention by a natural person.

Information system: A collection of programs and devices used to create, extract, send, receive, display, process, or store information.

Data message: The information created, sent, processed, received, stored, or displayed by one or more information systems or by electronic communication means.

Access to information: The ability to view, retrieve, use, or obtain information.

Originator of a data message: The person who, or on whose behalf, creates, sends, or stores a data message; however, a person acting as an intermediary in relation to that message shall not be deemed the originator of the data message.

Addressee: The person to whom the originator of the data message intends to deliver the message; a person acting as an intermediary in relation to that message shall not be deemed the addressee.

Information: Data in the form of texts, codes, drawings, images, words, or sounds.

Personal information: Data about a person whose identity is determined or can reasonably be determined, whether from that data or by combining it with any other data.

Electronic signature: What is placed on a data message in the form of letters, numbers, symbols, signs, or otherwise, with a unique character, used to identify the signer and distinguish him from others, and for the purpose of indicating the signer's consent to the data message.

Signature creation information: The information, codes, or Encryption keys used by the signer to create the electronic signature.

Signer: The person legally entitled to access the signature creation information, who acts either for himself or on behalf of a person he represents, to use this information to create the electronic signature.

Certification service provider: A person licensed to maintain a public key infrastructure, issue Certification Certificates, and provide services related to electronic signatures.

Certification Certificate: A document issued by the Certification service provider confirming the validity of the link between the signer and the signature creation information.

Relying party: The person who acts based on a Certification Certificate or an electronic signature.

Electronic transaction: Any dealing, contract, or agreement, concluded or executed, either partially or wholly, by means of electronic communications.

E-commerce service: A service provided, usually for a fee or of a non-commercial nature, delivered through a combination of an information system and any wired or wireless Telecom Services network, including e-government services.

Service provider: The person who provides the e-commerce service.

Place of business: The permanent establishments or facilities used to conduct business, including the provision of any service used exclusively for this purpose.

Client: A person conducting a transaction as part of an e-commerce service.

Consumer: A person acting for purposes other than those of his trade, profession, or business.

Telecommunications, whether wired or wireless: Sending, broadcasting, or receiving signals, texts, forms, sounds, images, data, or information of any kind, by wired, radio, optical, or any other electromagnetic communications, or by any other similar means of Telecommunications.

Wired or wireless telecommunications network: A wired, radio, optical, or other electromagnetic communication system used to transmit, switch, or send wired or wireless Telecommunications Services between endpoints in the network, including fixed and mobile terrestrial networks, satellite networks, electricity transmission systems, and other transmission systems to the extent they are used for wired or wireless Telecommunications and circuit- or packet-switched networks, including those used for Internet Protocol services, and networks used to provide broadcasting services, including cable television networks.

Wired or wireless telecommunications service: A form of transmission for signals, texts, or forms or others, by means of a wired or wireless telecommunications network, except for radio and television broadcasting services.

Internet Protocol: Any set of communication protocols that define interoperability communication standards for the internet network and transmissions, and other related applications, including Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) and the TCP/IP protocol suite.

Hosting services: Electronic services provided to users allowing them to store information on the service provider's information systems so that it may be accessed by other e-commerce service users.

Caching: The temporary storage of information in one or more information systems, where the information is stored for the purpose of frequent access.

## Chapter Two (2-3)

### Application of the Law (2-3)

#### Article 2

The provisions of this Law / Act apply to transactions conducted between persons who have agreed to carry out their transactions using Telecommunications.

Consent of a person to conduct transactions using Telecommunications may be inferred from their affirmative conduct.

The consent of government entities and agencies must be explicit in relation to electronic transactions in which they are involved.

The competent government entities and agencies, if they decide to carry out any of their tasks via Telecommunications, may specify any additional Specifications or conditions.

#### Article 3

The provisions of this Law / Act shall not apply to the following documents, records, and transactions:

1- Documents and records related to family and personal status matters.

2- Deleted.

3- Deleted.

4- Negotiable commercial papers in accordance with the provisions of the Commercial Law / Act.

Based on a proposal from the Supreme Council, the Cabinet / Council of Ministers may, by a decision and for considerations of public interest, remove from or add to the exceptions stipulated in the previous paragraph.

## Chapter Three (4-19)

### Requirements of Electronic Transactions (4-19)

#### Article 4

When concluding contracts or conducting transactions, the offer or acceptance may be expressed, in whole or in part, by a data message sent via electronic communications.

The use of one or more data messages in concluding contracts or transactions does not affect their validity or enforceability.

#### Article 5

A data message is considered to be sent by the originator if he sends it himself. It is also considered to be sent by him in the following cases:

1- If the data message is sent by a person authorized to act on behalf of the originator, in relation to a data message, if it is sent from an information system or an automated messaging system programmed and operated by or on behalf of the originator.

2- If the recipient correctly applies a procedure previously approved by the originator for the purpose of verifying that the data message was sent by the originator.

3- If the data message received by the recipient has resulted from the actions of a person who, by lawful means and by virtue of their relationship with the originator or his representative, was able to access a method used by the originator to identify the data message as sent by him.

#### Article 6

The data message is not considered to be issued by the originator in the following two cases:

1- If the recipient receives a notification from the originator indicating that the data message was not issued by them, provided that the recipient has been given a reasonable period of time to act accordingly.

2- If the recipient knows, or should have known, after exercising reasonable care or using any agreed-upon procedure, that the data message was not issued by the originator.

#### Article 7

The recipient may, within the framework of the relationship between him and the originator, rely on the data message sent by the originator and act on this basis.

The recipient may not rely on the aforementioned data message if he knows, or ought to know, if he exercises reasonable care or uses any agreed procedure, that this message, as received, is the result of an error in the process of Telecommunications.

#### Article 8

The recipient has the right to consider each data message received as an independent data message and to act accordingly, unless it is a duplicate of another data message and the recipient knew, or should have known, this by exercising reasonable care or by using any procedure agreed upon with the originator.

#### Article 9

If the originator has requested or agreed with the recipient, before or at the time of sending the data message, to send him an acknowledgment of receipt, the data message is considered received by the recipient when the originator receives the aforementioned acknowledgment. This does not implicitly mean that the data message sent matches the message received.

#### Article 10

If the originator has not specified or agreed with the recipient that acknowledgment of receipt of the data message should be in a specific form or by a particular method, the recipient may acknowledge receipt by any automated communication, by other means, or by conduct, as long as it is sufficient to inform the originator that the data message has been received.

#### Article 11

If the originator has stated that the data message is conditional upon receipt of an acknowledgment of receipt, it shall be deemed not to have been sent by the originator until the acknowledgment is received.

If the originator has not stated that the data message is conditional upon receipt of an acknowledgment, and such acknowledgment has not been received, the originator may send a notice to the recipient stating that the previous data message requires an acknowledgment of receipt, and specifying a reasonable period within which the acknowledgment must be received. If the acknowledgment is not received within the specified period after notification to the recipient, the originator may consider the data message as if it had not been sent, or may exercise any other rights he may have.

#### Article 12

When the acknowledgment of receipt states that the data message has met the technical requirements, whether agreed upon or specified in established standards, it is presumed that those requirements have been met.

#### Article 13

The provisions of Articles (9), (10), (11), (12) of this Law are limited to the إثبات of the sending or receipt of a data message, and these provisions do not extend to addressing the legal effects that may result from the data message itself or the acknowledgment of its receipt.

#### Article 14

Unless the originator and the recipient agree on a specific time for sending the data message, the time of sending shall be determined as follows:

1- When it enters an information system outside the control of the originator.

2- When it enters the first information system, if the data message is entered sequentially into more than one information system outside the control of the originator.

#### Article 15

Unless otherwise agreed between the sender and the recipient, the time of receipt of a data message is determined as follows:

1- When the recipient specifies an electronic address for receipt, the time of receipt is the time when the data message becomes accessible to the recipient at that electronic address.

2- If the data message is sent to an electronic address belonging to the recipient other than the specified address, the time of receipt is the time when it becomes accessible, with the recipient being aware that the data message has been sent to this address, or the time when the recipient retrieves the data message, whichever is earlier.

#### Article 16

Unless otherwise agreed between the originator and the recipient:

1- The data message is considered to have been sent from the place where the originator’s place of business is located, and it is considered to have been received at the place where the recipient’s place of business is located.

2- If the originator or recipient has more than one place of business, the relevant place of business shall be the one most closely connected to the transaction in question.

3- If the originator or recipient has more than one place of business, and none of them meet the criteria in the previous paragraph, the principal place of business shall be used.

4- If the originator or recipient does not have a place of business, habitual residence shall be considered.

#### Article 17

A place is not considered a place of business merely because of the presence of equipment and technology supporting the information system used there or any other Part of an information system used by a party to the transaction, or where other parties can access the said information system.

#### Article 18

The mere use by a person of a domain name or email address associated with a particular State is not considered evidence that their place of business is located in that State.

#### Article 19

If a natural person enters information unintentionally or makes a mistake during the entry process in a data message exchanged with an automated messaging system of another party that does not provide the person with an opportunity to correct the error, the individual who entered the information, or anyone acting on their behalf, has the right to withdraw the portion of the data message where the mistaken entry occurred, according to the following two conditions:

1- Informing the other party of the error as soon as possible after becoming aware of it.

2- Not using the goods or services, or obtaining any benefit or material value from them, in cases where the mistaken entry relates to goods or services.

## Chapter Four (20-27)

### Effects and Authenticity of Electronic Transactions (20-27)

#### Article 20

The information contained in a data message does not lose its legal effect, validity, or enforceability simply because it is in the form of a data message.

Similarly, this information does not lose its legal effect, validity, or enforceability if it is presented in a summarized form, provided that the data message clearly indicates how to access the details of that information, and that access is available in a way that allows the information to be used and referred to later by anyone entitled to access and use such information, and the method of access does not impose an unreasonable burden on them.

#### Article 21

If the Law / Act requires any document or transaction to be in writing, or imposes certain consequences for not complying with this requirement, the document or transaction meets this condition if it is in the form of a data message, provided that it can be accessed in a manner that allows it to be used for reference.

#### Article 22

If the Law / Act requires a signature on a document or record or transaction, or assigns legal effects to the lack of signature, the electronic signature that meets the conditions specified in Article (28) of this Law / Act, satisfies this requirement.

#### Article 23

If the Law / Act requires the provision or retention of information in its original form, or if it prescribes legal effects for failing to do so, then providing or retaining it in the form of a data message meets this requirement, when the following are achieved:

1- It must be reasonably possible to demonstrate the integrity and reliability of the information, from the time it was first created in its final form as a data message, until the time it is accessed and displayed.

2- The standard for assessing the integrity of the data message, according to the previous clause, is to determine whether it remains complete and unchanged, except for any alteration resulting from communication, storage, or display of the data message, which does not affect its content. The degree of reliability of the information is assessed in light of the purpose for which the data message was created and all other relevant circumstances.

3- The ability to access the data message in a manner that allows its use and reference by any person entitled to access and use it.

#### Article 24

If the Law requires the retention of any information, document, or record, or imposes legal consequences for failure to do so, retaining it in the form of a data message fulfills this requirement, provided that the following are met:

1- The ability to access the data message in a way that allows its use and reference by any person entitled to access and use it.

2- Retaining the data message in the original form in which it was created, sent, or received, or in a form that enables Evidence / Proof that it accurately represents the information contained in the data message, as it was created, sent, or received in its original form.

3- Retaining information of the data message that enables the identification of its source, destination, and the date and time of its sending or receipt, if available.

#### Article 25

The acceptance of the document, record, or transaction as Evidence / Proof is not prevented by its being in the form of a data message, even if it is not in its original form, if it is the only Evidence / Proof that can be obtained by the person relying on it.

#### Article 26

When assessing the evidential value for information, documents, or records in the form of a data message, the following must be taken into account:

1- The procedures and circumstances under which the data message was created, stored, or transmitted.

2- The procedures and circumstances under which the integrity of the document, record, or information contained in the data message was preserved.

3- The procedures and circumstances under which the originator of the data message was identified.

4- Any other relevant procedures or circumstances.

#### Article 27

The validity or enforceability of the Contract concluded through interaction between an automated messaging system and a natural person, or through interaction between automated messaging systems, may not be denied solely because a natural person did not review or intervene in each of the actions carried out by the automated messaging systems or in the Contract resulting from those actions.

## Chapter Five (28-34)

### Electronic Signature (28-34)

#### Article 28

The electronic signature shall have evidentiary authority if it meets the following conditions:

1- The signature creation information is linked to the signatory and not to any other person.

2- The signature creation information, at the time of signing, is under the control of the signatory and not any other person.

3- It must be possible to detect any alteration made to the electronic signature after the signing.

4- It must be possible to detect any alteration made to the information of the data message after the time of signing, if the purpose of requiring the signature by law is to assure the integrity of the information to which the signature relates.

The Supreme Council shall issue the necessary decisions to define which electronic signature processes and technologies fulfill the above provisions.

#### Article 29

The signer must, when creating an electronic signature, comply with the following:

1- Exercise reasonable care to avoid the unauthorized use of signature creation information.

2- Use the means provided by the certification service provider, according to the provisions of Articles (36), (37) of this Law / Act without delay, to notify any person whom the signer assumes is relying on the electronic signature, as well as to take the necessary measures to strengthen the electronic signature, in the event that the signature creation information is exposed or there is a strong probability that it is exposed to suspicion.

3- Exercise a reasonable degree of care, when using the certification Testimony to strengthen the electronic signature, to ensure the accuracy and completeness of all material confirmations provided by the signer relevant to the certification Testimony throughout its validity period or intended to be included in the certification Testimony.

#### Article 30

The signatory shall bear the legal consequences arising from their failure to fulfill the obligations referred to in the previous article.

#### Article 31

The party relying on the electronic signature bears the legal consequences resulting from its failure to take reasonable steps to verify that the electronic signature meets the requirements set forth in Article (28) of this Law / Act, or to verify the validity of the certification Testimony, its source, suspension, revocation, or any restriction on it in case the electronic signature is supported by a certification Testimony.

#### Article 32

The electronic signature is considered legally valid, regardless of the geographical location where the signature is created or used, or the geographical location of the signer's workplace.

#### Article 33

An electronic signature created or used outside the State shall have the same legal effect within it, if it provides an equivalent level of reliability, not less than the level required under Article (28) of this Law / Act.

#### Article 34

Subject to the provisions of Article (28) of this Law / Act, the parties may agree to use specific types of electronic signatures, provided that the agreement is valid in accordance with the Law / Act.

## Chapter Six (35-44)

### Certification Service (35-44)

#### Article 35

The certification service provider, when delivering services to enhance the electronic signature, must comply with the following:

1- Act in accordance with the information provided about the practice of its activity.

2- Exercise a reasonable degree of care to ensure the accuracy and completeness of all material information provided relevant to the certification certificate, or the information included therein, throughout its validity period.

3- Use trustworthy systems, procedures, and human resources in performing its services, in accordance with the standards and regulations specified by a decision issued by the Supreme Council.

#### Article 36

The certification service provider must provide the signer with a means to notify them if the signature creation information has been subject to any suspicious activity, and must ensure that a signature revocation service is available for timely use.

#### Article 37

The certification service provider must provide means, reasonably accessible, that enable the relying party on its services to ensure that the Testimony contains the following:

1- The identity of the certification service provider.

2- That the signer was in control of the signature creation information at the time of issuing the Testimony.

3- That the signature creation information was accurate at the time of issuing the Testimony.

#### Article 38

The certification service provider must provide the relying party with means, reasonably accessible, that enable the relying party to verify the following:

1- Identification of the certification service provider.

2- Existence of any restriction on the purpose or value for which the signature creation information or the certification شهادة may be used.

3- The method used in identifying the signer.

4- That the signature creation information is correct and has not been subjected to any suspicion.

5- Existence of any restriction on the scope or extent of the liability stipulated for the certification service provider.

6- The means to provide notification in accordance with this Law / Act.

7- Whether there is an instant service for revoking the signature.

#### Article 39

The certification service provider must cancel the Testimony of certification, or suspend it upon the request of its owner, or in other cases that necessitate it. A decision is issued by the Supreme Council to determine all these cases and their regulations.

The certification service provider must also immediately notify the owner of the Testimony of certification of the cancellation, suspension, the reason for that, and the removal of any of them if its reason ceases to exist.

The certification service provider is responsible for any damage that may occur to any bona fide person as a result of failing to cancel or suspend the Testimony of certification in the cases stipulated in paragraph one of this article.

#### Article 40

The certification service provider bears the legal consequences arising from its failure to fulfill the obligations stipulated in the previous articles of this chapter, including but not limited to, liability for damages incurred by any person who reasonably relies on the issued Testimony of certification and suffers harm as a result. When determining this liability, the following factors shall be considered:

1- The cost of obtaining the Testimony of certification.

2- The nature of the certified information.

3- The extent of any limitation on the purpose for which the Testimony of certification is used.

4- The existence of any statement or agreement limiting the liability of the certification service provider.

5- Any wrongful act by the relying party, including negligence or misconduct.

#### Article 41

When determining whether the Testimony of authentication is legally valid, the geographical location in which it is issued, or where the issuer's place of business is located, is not taken into account.

#### Article 42

A Ratification certificate issued outside the State has the same legal effect within it, provided that it is issued by an accredited Ratification service provider, and offers a level of reliability equivalent to at least the level stipulated in articles (35), (36), (37), (38) of this Law / Act.

The Supreme Council determines the controls and procedures required for approving Ratification certificates issued by foreign entities outside the State.

#### Article 43

Individuals may agree to use specific types of authentication certificates, provided that the agreement is valid according to the law.

#### Article 44

The Supreme Council issues the regulations and decisions necessary to organize the work of certification service providers, in particular the following:

1- Controls and conditions for issuing the necessary Licenses to practice the activity of certification service providers, their renewal, suspension, the procedures required to obtain the License, its duration, renewal, suspension, cancellation, and transfer, the obligations of the licensee, controls and procedures for suspending the activity of the licensee, and the consequences thereof.

2- Certification programs for certification service providers.

3- Standards of form and content for certification certificates, and other practices or procedures related to certification services.

4- Prescribed fees for certification service providers, and rules for determining the fees for services provided by them.

5- Submission of reports or other notification procedures.

6- Fines and penalties prescribed for violating the rules governing the work of certification service providers.

## Chapter Seven (45-50)

### Transmission and Storage of Information (45-50)

#### Article 45

The provider of electronic commerce services is not held responsible for sending information of the electronic commerce services provided or requested by the user, or for providing access to the Telecommunications network or service, in the following cases:

1- The provider does not initiate the transmission.

2- The provider does not select the recipient of the transmission.

3- The provider does not select or modify the information contained in the transmission.

The transmission and access provision referred to in the previous paragraph includes the automatic, intermediate, and transient storage of transmitted information, when this is done solely for the purpose of transmission through the Telecommunications network, and provided that the information is not stored longer than is reasonably necessary for the transmission.

#### Article 46

The service provider is not liable for automatic, intermediate, and transient storage of electronic commerce service information provided by the service user, which has been transmitted through a network or Telecommunications service, in the following cases:

1- When the storage is done for the purpose of making the transmission of information more effective for other service users, based on their request.

2- The service provider’s adherence to the following:

a- Not making any changes to the information.

b- Complying with the conditions for accessing the information.

c- Complying with the rules that relate to updating the information in the manner commonly recognized and used by similar service providers.

d- Adhering to not interfering in the lawful use of recognized technology, used by similar service providers, to obtain data about the use of the information.

e- Acting without delay to remove or disable access to the stored information when the service provider is actually aware that the information at the primary transmission source has been removed from the network or access to it has been disabled, or that a court or competent government authority has ordered such removal or disabling.

3- Or when the end user chooses caching in their use of an electronic commerce service that essentially alters the caching settings generated by the service provider.

#### Article 47

The hosting service provider is not held liable for those services in the following cases:

1- Lack of actual knowledge of the illegal activity or information associated with certain hosting services, or lack of awareness of facts or circumstances indicating that such information or activity was illegal.

2- Acting without delay to remove or disable access to the affected information or services upon becoming aware of the illegality of the activity or information associated with certain hosting services.

3- If the actions of the user of the hosting service are not carried out with the provider's consent or under their control.

#### Article 48

The provisions of articles (45), (46), and (47) above shall not prejudice any legal obligations arising from any contract.

#### Article 49

In applying the provisions of Clause (2/e) of Article (46) and Clause (1) of Article (47) above, the actual knowledge of the provider of e-commerce or hosting services, as the case may be, is determined through all relevant matters and circumstances, including whether they have

received

any notice

containing

the following:

1- The name and full address of the sender of the notice.

2- Details of the location of the relevant information.

3- Details of the nature of the illegal activity or information.

#### Article 50

The provisions of the previous articles in this chapter do not prejudice the right of the competent government authorities, in accordance with the prescribed legal procedures, to require the E-Commerce service provider or hosting services to take specific measures by notifying them of any illegal activities or information, obliging them to provide those authorities with any information to identify the user involved in the illegal activities and information.

## Chapter Eight (51-59)

### Consumer Protection (51-59)

#### Article 51

Without prejudice to the provisions of Law No. (8) of 2008 regarding Consumer Protection, the service provider must make available to the consumers of its services and any competent government authority, in a form and manner that can be easily, directly, and continuously accessed, the following information:

1- Name of the service provider.

2- Address of the service provider.

3- Contact information related to the service provider, including electronic communication address.

4- Commercial Register data, or any other similar means, to identify the service provider, if registered in a Commercial Register or similar publicly accessible register.

5- Information of the Competent Authority that supervises the service provider, whenever the provision of the service is subject to authorization or license from it.

6- Statement of the codes or rules of conduct that the service provider is subject to, and the possibility and method of accessing them electronically.

7- Any other information the Supreme Council deems important for the protection of consumers of electronic commerce services.

#### Article 52

The service provider who practices a profession subject to specific regulation and requires a license or permit to practice it must provide the following:

1- The details of the authority or professional institution with which the service provider is registered.

2- The professional title in use and the name of the State in which the title was granted.

3- The professional rules or other regulations applicable to the service provider in the State granting the license or permit, and how to access them.

4- Any other information that the Supreme Council deems important for the protection of electronic commerce service consumers.

#### Article 53

Any electronic communication whose subject is an e-commerce service of a commercial nature, or that constitutes part of such a service and is provided by the service provider, must meet the following conditions:

1- It must be clear that the communication is of a commercial nature.

2- The person on whose behalf the communication is made must be clearly identified.

3- For promotional offers or contests, the following are required:

A- They must be specified accurately and clearly.

B- They must indicate whether they include discounts, rewards, or gifts.

C- The conditions required for participation must not be misleading or deceptive, must be stated clearly and without ambiguity, and must be easily accessible.

4- It must not violate public order or public morals.

#### Article 54

The service provider may not send, or instruct others to send, electronic Telecommunications of a commercial nature to any consumer from whom explicit consent to such sending has not been obtained.

The consumer’s consent to the sending is presumed in the presence of a relationship with the service provider that gives a clear expectation from the consumer to receive the electronic communication, provided that the content of the electronic communication is relevant to the purpose for which this relationship was established, and on the condition that the service provider provides the opportunity and appropriate means for the recipient of the electronic communication to request not to receive further electronic Telecommunications at any time.

The Supreme Council may issue additional rules related to unsolicited electronic Telecommunications.

#### Article 55

If the electronic communication relates to a request to conclude a Contract of a commercial nature, and before the request is submitted, the service provider must clearly and comprehensively provide the consumer with a full statement of the terms and conditions of the Contract, including:

1- The technical steps required to conclude the Contract.

2- The information related to the service provider.

3- A description of the essential characteristics of the services or goods.

4- The price of the services and goods, indicating whether it includes taxes and delivery costs.

5- The arrangements related to payment, delivery, and execution.

6- The duration of validity of the offer and the price.

7- Whether the consumer has the right to cancel the request.

8- Whether the intended Contract to be concluded will be stored or retained by the service provider, and the consumer's ability to access it, store it, copy it, and retain it, and the methods to do so.

#### Article 56

The service provider, if a consumer of the e-commerce service submits their request through Telecommunications, must adhere to the following:

1- Providing appropriate, effective, and accessible means that allow the service consumer to identify and correct input errors before submitting the request.

2- Acknowledging receipt of the request from the service consumer without undue delay and using appropriate electronic Telecommunications.

The request or acknowledgment is considered received when the intended parties are able to access them, and the acknowledgment of receipt may be in the form of providing a paid service if this service is an e-commerce service.

Non-consumer parties may agree otherwise.

#### Article 57

Unless the parties agree otherwise, the consumer, in the case of concluding Contracts via electronic Telecommunications, has the right to Contract Rescission or termination within three (3) days from the date of concluding the Contract, as long as the Contract has not been fully performed by the service provider during this period in a manner that fulfills its purpose, and the consumer has not used the products or goods received or derived any benefit or material value from them.

#### Article 58

Unless the service provider and the consumer agree on another delivery or execution period for the Contract, the consumer may proceed with Contract Termination with the service provider if there is a delay in delivery or execution of the Contract for a period exceeding thirty (30) days, and recover any amounts paid under the Contract for products, goods, services, or other obligations of the Contract affected by this delay.

The consumer is not obligated to pay for any products, goods, or services not requested, or to pay the cost of returning them, including any products or goods delivered by the service provider by mistake.

The service provider is required to notify the consumer of any delays or other difficulties encountered that have a material impact on the execution of the Contract.

#### Article 59

The service provider must specify the purposes for which personal information about the client is collected, at or before collecting this information, and is not permitted to collect, use, retain, or disclose the client's personal information for purposes not authorized or permitted, unless required or permitted by Law / Act, or with the consent of the client to whom the personal information relates.

The service provider is responsible for any records containing the client's personal information or any records of the client's electronic communications, which are in the custody, under the control, or with agents of the service provider.

The service provider must take reasonable steps to ensure that the client's personal information, and related records, are protected in a manner that is secure and appropriate to their importance.

## Chapter Nine (60-66)

### Powers of the Supreme Council (60-66)

#### Article 60

The Supreme Council, as the highest Power specialized in matters of Telecommunications Regulation and Information Technology (IT), shall work to enable the use of electronic transactions and electronic commerce in a facilitated manner, and for achieving this purpose, in particular, it shall carry out the following:

1- Supervision of the provision, use, and development of means for electronic transactions and electronic commerce.

2- Issuing Licenses and permits as required In accordance with the provisions of this Law, renewing them, suspending their enforcement, and revoking them.

3- Supervising the development of codes and rules of conduct for the Information Technology (IT) sector and practices of service providers.

4- Taking the appropriate legal procedures and measures against service providers and other persons subject to the provisions of this Law, to ensure their commitment and adherence to its provisions, the regulations, and implementing decisions.

5- Establishing controls, standards, and necessary systems for information protection, including the client’s personal information.

6- Establishing appropriate principles, controls, and standards for protecting consumers who use electronic transactions or electronic commerce services.

7- Issuing decisions to determine Licenses fees, permits, and services provided by the Supreme Council, as well as rules for estimating the consideration for services, In accordance with the provisions of this Law.

#### Article 61

The Supreme Council shall be solely responsible for managing the top-level domains for the State of Qatar’s country code on the Internet, and it may grant Authorization / Delegation to others in this regard.

The Supreme Council shall issue the necessary decisions regarding the management and mechanisms of domain names in the State on the Internet, including the imposition of any service charges or fees related thereto, and the establishment of dispute resolution procedures related to domain names.

#### Article 62

The Supreme Council may request service providers or others

concerned

in the field of electronic transactions and commerce to provide the information necessary to exercise its powers, in the manner, form, and within the time it specifies.

#### Article 63

The regulations, decisions, orders, and rules issued by the Supreme Council, in accordance with the provisions of this Law, must be characterized by transparency and non-discrimination between service providers and other participants in the field of electronic transactions and commerce.

It is not considered discrimination to take any decisions in accordance with the provisions of this Law that may have a different effect on any service provider or any other participant in the field of electronic transactions and commerce, whenever this is due to the objectively different circumstances of each of them.

#### Article 64

A Committee called "Grievances and Dispute Settlement Committee" is established by the Supreme Council, formed of a Chairman / President and a number of members who have expertise.

experienced

A resolution is issued by the Board of Directors of the Supreme Council to Nominating the Chairman / President and members.

Additionally, a resolution by the Board of Directors of the Supreme Council is issued regarding the Committee’s operating system and the procedures followed before it.

#### Article 65

The Appeals and Dispute Settlement Committee is responsible for the following:

1- Adjudicating appeals against decisions issued by the Supreme Council In accordance with the provisions of this Law.

2- Settling disputes that may arise between service providers, In accordance with the provisions of this Law.

3- Settling disputes that may arise between service providers and their clients, In accordance with the provisions of this Law.

#### Article 66

The Decision issued by the Committee for Grievances and Dispute Settlement shall be final, and concerned parties may submit a Legal Challenge / Appeal against it before the Administrative Circuit at the Court of First Instance.

A Lawsuit / Legal Action regarding any of the grievances or disputes mentioned in the previous article shall not be accepted except after a Decision has been issued by the Committee, or sixty days have passed from the date of its submission to the Committee without being decided, whichever is earlier.

## Chapter Ten (67-73)

### Offences and Penalties (67-73)

#### Article 67

Without prejudice to any stricter Penalty stipulated by any other Law / Act, imprisonment for a period not exceeding two years, and a fine not exceeding (300,000 riyals) three hundred thousand riyals, or one of these Penalties, shall be imposed on any person who intentionally commits one or more of the following acts:

1- Unauthorized access to any information system, data message, e-commerce service, or related transaction, including bypassing technical security measures, with the intent to obtain information or other unauthorized use of the information system, data message, e-commerce service, or related transaction.

2- Providing false or misleading information to the Supreme Council, or misuse of Ratification services.

3- Creating, publishing, or using electronic signatures or Ratification Certificates for unlawful purposes.

4- Destroying or impairing a data message, electronic signature, Ratification Certificate, or any other electronic medium.

5- Forging a data message, electronic signature, Ratification Certificate, or any other electronic medium, by fabrication, modification, alteration, or any other method, or using any of them with knowledge thereof.

6- Providing false information to a Ratification service provider, or false electronic signature information to any party relying on such signature under this Law / Act.

7- Unauthorized access, copying, restructuring, or obtaining an electronic signature system or electronic signature creation information belonging to another person.

8- Impersonating a person or claiming representation when submitting an application to obtain a Ratification Certificate, accepting it, requesting its suspension, or cancellation.

9- Publishing, Circular, or providing a Ratification Certificate

contains

or refers to incorrect data.

10- Hacking or unlawful interference with any information system, electronic communication, or e-commerce service.

11- Operating as a Ratification service provider without obtaining a License for this from the Supreme Council.

12- Violating any provision of Articles (51), (52), (53), (54), (55), (59) of this Law / Act.

#### Article 68

The Court shall, in the case of Conviction under the provisions of this Law / Act, in addition to any Penalty it deems appropriate, decide upon the confiscation of the tools used in the commission of the Crime.

The Court may rule to publish the Conviction Ruling in two widely circulated daily newspapers and on open electronic information networks, at the expense of the convicted person.

#### Article 69

The Official responsible for the actual Department / Directorate management of the legal person shall be punished with the same penalties prescribed for acts committed in violation of the provisions of this Law / Act, if it is proven that he was aware of it, or if his failure to fulfill the duties imposed on him by that Department / Directorate contributed to the occurrence of the Crime.

#### Article 70

In the event of a Judgment of conviction under the provisions of the previous article, the legal person to whom the convicted person belongs shall be sentenced to the Fine stipulated in article (67) of this Law / Act, or to a Fine equal to the value of the Fine imposed on the Official in actual management in case he is punished with a Fine without imprisonment.

#### Article 71

The Penalty / Sentence is doubled in the case of recidivism, and a recidivist is considered to be anyone who commits any of the Crimes specified in this Law / Act within three years from the date of completion of the execution of the Penalty / Sentence imposed or its lapse by the passage of time.

#### Article 72

Employees of the Supreme Council, who are granted the Capacity of Law enforcement Officer by a decision from the Attorney General, in coordination with the Supreme Council, shall have the authority to detect and establish acts committed in violation of the provisions of this Law / Act and the decisions issued in implementation thereof.

For this purpose, they are authorized to enter relevant places, review electronic records, documents, and equipment, as well as any other related items, and to request data and clarifications they deem necessary, and to prepare reports documenting acts that occur at these sites.

#### Article 73

The provisions of this section apply to anyone who:

1- Committed an act outside Qatar that makes him a perpetrator or accomplice in a Crime that occurred wholly or partly inside Qatar.

2- Committed an act inside Qatar that makes him a perpetrator or accomplice in a Crime that occurred wholly or partly outside Qatar, provided that it is punishable under this Law / Act and the law of the country in which the Crime occurred.
